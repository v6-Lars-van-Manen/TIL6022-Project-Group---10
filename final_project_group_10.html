Project Group 10 – Rail Sabotage and Road Traffic on the A1¶

Did the railway disruptions of 15 September 2026 change road traffic on the A1 between Amersfoort, Apeldoorn and Deventer?

Members: Lars van Manen, Jente van den Berg, Vigo Groenewold, Sipke de Jong, Jasper Steenvoorden

TIL6022 TIL Python Programming – Final project

Motivation and introduction¶

Rail and road transport networks are closely interconnected. When railway services are disrupted, travellers may choose alternative modes of transport, change their route, or cancel their trip altogether. As a result, railway disruptions can increase pressure on parts of the road network (Huang & Loo, 2023).

The railwayline between Amersfoort, Apeldoorn and Deventer links the Randstad to the eastern Netherlands, and its services are regularly disrupted. On 15 September 2026 (Prinsjesdag), objects deliberately placed on the tracks at 35 or more locations caused disruptions to train traffic in the centre and east of the country (NOS, 2026). The Amersfoort–Deventer corridor was disrupted too; that evening, another obstruction near Teuge halted trains between Apeldoorn and Deventer (NOS, 2026).

While the direct effects of the railway disruptions were widely reported (NOS, 2026), their potential impact on road traffic remains unclear. This project investigates whether the railway disruptions of 15 September 2026 were associated with measurable changes in traffic conditions on the A1 corridor between Amersfoort, Apeldoorn and Deventer. NDW traffic data will be used to compare traffic intensity and average vehicle speed on the disruption day with those on comparable Tuesdays.

Research Objective¶

Research question

To what extent did the railway disruptions on the Amersfoort–Apeldoorn–Deventer corridor on 15 September 2026 lead to measurable changes in road traffic intensity and average vehicle speed on the parallel A1 corridor?

Objectives

  • Construct a baseline traffic profile for the A1 between Amersfoort and Deventer from historical NDW measurements.
  • Quantify how traffic intensity and average speed on 15 September 2026 deviated from that profile, per location, direction and hour.
  • Quantify the severity of the rail disruptions on the Amersfoort–Apeldoorn–Deventer corridor using train service cancellation data.
  • Determine whether the deviations in traffic intensity and average speed coincide in time and place with the rail disruptions.

Understanding how the road network responds to rail disruptions may help road authorities such as Rijkswaterstaat anticipate and manage the wider impacts of future disruptions. The findings may also contribute to a better understanding of the interaction between rail and road transport networks.

Geographical and temporal scale¶

Geographical scale: The A1 motorway between Amersfoort, Apeldoorn and Deventer, which runs parallel to the disrupted railway. NDW measurement locations are selected along this section, in both driving directions and at several points along the corridor. The disrupted railway between the stations Amersfoort Centraal, Deventer and Apeldoorn.

Temporal scale: The disruption day, Tuesday 15 September 2026, analysed per hour. The baseline consists of the three Tuesdays before and the three Tuesdays after the disruption day.

Apart from the measurement locations, a relevant time period must be selected that accurately depicts what the expected car traffic on the A1 would have been on September 15 2026.

It is commonly known that car traffic varies based on the day of the week(CROW,2022). Since September 15 2026 was a Tuesday, this study will focus on Tuesdays. Moreover, to minimize macroeconomic confounding trends and seasonality, the study will more specifically focus on Tuesdays up to 3 weeks prior and 3 weeks after September 15 2026.

Rijden de Treinen Open Data¶

Rijden de Treinen. (n.d.). Open Data: Train services dataset. Retrieved from https://www.rijdendetreinen.nl/open-data/treinarchief#downloads

To quantify the severity of the railway disruptions, train service data from Rijden de Treinen was used. The dataset contains information on scheduled train services, cancellations and delays at individual stations. For this study, only long-distance passenger services calling at Amersfoort Centraal, Apeldoorn and Deventer were considered.

Local stopping services and replacement buses were excluded because the objective is to analyse disruptions affecting travellers who are most likely to use the A1 corridor as an alternative.

Data pipeline¶

The data analysis pipeline will be used to determine whether the railway disruption on 15 September 2026 resulted in measurable changes in road traffic along the A1 corridor between Amersfoort, Apeldoorn and Deventer. Historical traffic measurements from NDW Dexter will be used to compare the disruption day with normal traffic conditions.

1. Data collection¶

Traffic data will be collected from several NDW measurement locations along the A1 between Amersfoort, Apeldoorn and Deventer. Where possible, measurement points in both driving directions and at multiple locations along the corridor will be included.

For each measurement point, the main variables are:

  • traffic intensity in vehicles per hour;
  • average vehicle speed in km/h;
  • measurement location and direction;
  • time of day.

The disruption day, Tuesday 15 September 2026, will be analysed separately. To construct a representative baseline, the reference dataset will consist of all Tuesdays up to 3 week prior and 3 weeks after the disruption day.

In addition, train service data will be obtained from the Rijden de Treinen Open Data archive. The dataset contains information on scheduled train services, cancellations and delays at individual stations.

2. Data cleaning and pre-processing¶

The NDW datasets will be imported into Python and converted into a consistent format. Measurements from the different locations and dates will be combined into one dataset containing location, direction, date, time, traffic intensity and average speed.

Missing or technically unreliable measurements will be identified and removed where necessary. The reference data will then be filtered so that only eligible Tuesdays outside school holidays remain. Measurements will be aggregated into consistent hourly time intervals, allowing the disruption day and the baseline to be compared directly.

Train service records will be filtered to include only services calling at Amersfoort Centraal, Apeldoorn and Deventer. Furthermore, only long-distance passenger train categories (Intercity, Intercity Direct, ICE and Sneltrein) will be retained. For each service, the planned operating hour and disruption status will be extracted.

3. Baseline construction¶

For each measurement location, direction and hour of the day, the traffic measurements of all selected reference Tuesdays will be combined to create a normal traffic profile.

The mean traffic intensity and mean vehicle speed will be calculated for each hourly interval. This produces an expected value for the 15th of September.

For the railway data, a baseline will be constructed using the three Tuesdays before and the three Tuesdays after the disruption day. The average number of affected train services per hour will be calculated, providing a reference level of normal operational disturbances against which the disruption day can be compared.

4. Comparative analysis¶

Traffic conditions on 15 September 2026 will be compared with the baseline for the same location, direction and hour. For traffic intensity, both the absolute and the relative difference will be calculated:

$$\Delta I = I_{\text{disruption}} - I_{\text{baseline}}$$

Formula 1: absolute difference in traffic intensity

$$\%\Delta I = \frac{I_{\text{disruption}} - I_{\text{baseline}}}{I_{\text{baseline}}} \times 100$$

Formula 2: relative difference in traffic intensity

The same calculations will be performed for average vehicle speed.

A higher-than-normal traffic intensity may indicate additional road demand during the rail disruption, while a decrease in average speed may indicate additional congestion. Both variables will therefore be analysed together.

For train services, a service is classified as affected when it is either fully cancelled or partially cancelled. The number of affected services per hour on 15 September 2026 will be compared with the baseline level of affected services on comparable Tuesdays.

5. Spatial and temporal analysis¶

The calculated differences will be compared across locations and throughout the day. This makes it possible to investigate both when abnormal traffic conditions occurred and where along the corridor the largest deviations were observed.

Particular attention will be given to the time periods in which rail services were disrupted. The railway analysis makes it possible to identify when disruption levels were highest and whether these periods coincide with deviations in traffic intensity or average speed on the A1 corridor.

6. Visualization and interpretation¶

The results will be visualized using:

  • time-series plots comparing traffic conditions on the disruption day with the normal Tuesday baseline;
  • plots of the percentage change in traffic intensity and average speed;
  • a comparison of affected train services on the disruption day and the train disruption baseline;
  • comparisons between different measurement locations and directions.

These visualizations will be used to assess whether road traffic on 15 September differed systematically from typical Tuesday traffic and whether these differences coincide with the railway disruption.

Key Visualizations and Code Build-Up¶

Traffic speed and intensity¶

To visualize whether there has been a significant difference in traffic on the A1 highway, a python script has been made that follows 4 steps:

  • In step 0 the filepaths for the data files are fetched.

  • In step 1, the get_location_data_function function obtains the necessary data from the CSV files, this data consists of the hour-of-the-day, the traffic intensity of the specified hour and the average speed at the specified hour. The function reads the CSV files and converts them to dataframes. For some locations the traffic intensity and speed are already an aggregate of multiple sensors and can be directly passed to the dataframe. However, some locations have multiple measurement points and first need to be averaged before being passed to the dataframe. This function returns the data in a dictionary.

  • In step 2, the function plot_direction is created which first loads the data using the get_location_data function and secondly calculates the expected values by simply taking the average of the three weeks prior and the three weeks after.

  • Next up in step 3, the plot_direction function creates a 2x2 grid to plot the four graphs. At [0.0] and [0,1] the respective baseline traffic intensity and baseline average speed are plotted to give an identification how much variance there is between the weeks prior and the weeks after. This also makes it easy to observe any weird outliers in the data that have not been taking out in the initial data filtering. At [1,0] and [1,1] on the grid, the expected values for the traffic intensity and speed are plotted against the real values. In these two graphs any serious deviations between the expected and real values can be a consqequence of the train disruption, if this deviation can also be matched within a reasonable time period of the train disruptions.

Railway disruption analysis¶

To quantify the severity of the railway disruptions, a separate Python script was developed that follows three main steps:

  • In step 1, train service data from the Rijden de Treinen Open Data archive is loaded and filtered. Only train services calling at Amersfoort Centraal, Apeldoorn and Deventer are retained. Furthermore, only long-distance passenger services (Intercity, Intercity Direct, ICE and Sneltrein) are included in the analysis.
  • In step 2, the data is aggregated from individual station stops to unique train services. A train service is classified as affected when it is either fully cancelled or partially cancelled. For each service, the corresponding hour of operation is extracted, allowing disruptions to be analysed on an hourly basis.
  • In step 3, a disruption baseline is constructed using the three Tuesdays before and the three Tuesdays after 15 September 2026. The average number of affected train services per hour is calculated and compared with the disruption day itself. The results are visualized in a time-series plot showing the number of affected services on 15 September relative to normal operating conditions.

Analysis of Results¶

Railway Disruptions¶

To quantify the severity of the railway disruptions on 15 September 2026, train service data from Rijden de Treinen was analysed. The analysis focused on long-distance passenger services calling at Amersfoort Centraal, Apeldoorn and Deventer. After filtering, 523 unique train services remained in the dataset.

The disruption analysis shows that railway services on the corridor were severely affected throughout most of the day. Compared with the average number of affected services on the three Tuesdays before and after the disruption day, the number of disrupted services on 15 September was substantially higher during both the morning and afternoon periods.

The traffic intensity measurements generally followed the expected daily pattern, including the typical morning and afternoon peak periods. Comparison of Rail and Road Effects¶

The railway disruption analysis demonstrates that train services on the Amersfoort–Apeldoorn–Deventer corridor were heavily affected throughout the day. In contrast, the traffic data shows only limited deviations from normal traffic conditions on the parallel A1 motorway.

The peak level of rail disruption occurred at 09:00, when 27 of the 30 scheduled services (90%) were affected. During this same period, the observed traffic intensity and average speed on the A1 remained close to the baseline values and did not show a corresponding peak deviation.

Conclusion¶

The railway disruptions of 15 September 2026 caused major interruptions to train services on the Amersfoort–Apeldoorn–Deventer corridor. Analysis of train service data showed that the number of affected services was substantially higher than normal and that between 64% and 90% of scheduled long-distance train services were disrupted during large parts of the day.

Despite the severity of the railway disruption, traffic measurements on the A1 motorway showed little evidence of a corresponding increase in road traffic. Traffic intensity and average vehicle speeds generally remained close to their expected values, and no consistent corridor-wide pattern of increased road demand was observed.

The results therefore suggest that the railway disruptions of 15 September 2026 did not lead to a substantial increase in traffic on the studied section of the A1. Although individual travellers may have switched to car travel, the overall effect was too small to be clearly detected in the motorway traffic data.

Within the limitations of the available datasets, this study indicates that even severe railway disruptions do not necessarily translate into measurable increases in motorway traffic.

Discussion¶

There was not a clear increase in traffic on the A1 motorway. A possible explanation is that affected travellers did not simply switch from train to car. Instead, they may have postponed their trip, travelled at a different time, used replacement transport or chosen alternative rail routes. Because this study only observes traffic on the A1, these behavioural responses cannot be measured directly.

There are also a few limitations. Traffic conditions can be influenced by many factors, including accidents, roadworks and normal day-to-day fluctuations. Another limitation is that the train service dataset contains information on disrupted services but not on the number of passengers affected by each disruption.

Within the scope of this study, there is little evidence that the railway disruption resulted in a substantial increase in motorway traffic.

Future research could combine train service data with passenger counts or broader mobility datasets to better understand how travellers respond to disruptions in the rail network.
